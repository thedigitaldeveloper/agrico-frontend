Postgraduate agricultural social science degree

What Agricultural Extension Education studies

M.Sc. (Agriculture) Agricultural Extension Education is a social-science specialisation that studies the link between agricultural research and farming communities. Instead of a single crop or technology, it looks at people and institutions: how extension and advisory services are organised, how farmers make decisions, how innovations spread, and how training, media and digital tools can support change.

Agricultural universities generally follow the ICAR Broad Subject Matter Area (BSMA) PG syllabus for Social Sciences, adapted by each academic council. The BSMA extension courses move beyond the older transfer-of-technology model toward pluralistic advisory services, with attention to producer groups, gender, natural resource management, market access and climate change. Students also take minor and supporting courses, common courses and a seminar, and finish with a thesis based on field surveys or other social research methods.

TNAU offers the programme in its Department of Agricultural Extension and Rural Sociology at Coimbatore, and also at its Madurai and Killikulam agricultural colleges, describing a 70-credit load with 30 credits for thesis research. Many other state agricultural universities run similar programmes, sometimes under titles such as Extension Education or Agricultural Extension.

Who should apply for this programme

This programme is well-suited for students and aspirants who align with the following academic profile and career ambitions:

  • Agriculture and allied graduates more interested in working with farmers and communities than in laboratory research.
  • Students who enjoy communication, training, group facilitation and using media or digital tools.
  • Applicants curious about why technologies are or are not adopted, and how to measure programme impact.
  • Those aiming for KVK, rural development, NGO, farmer producer organisation or teaching and research roles.

Hands-on practical learning & experiential attachments

Agricultural degrees place heavy emphasis on experiential, field-based and industrial training to ensure students graduate with job-ready operational competencies:

Thesis research

A supervised social-science study, often based on structured surveys, interviews or scale development in farming communities, ending in a thesis and viva voce.

Field exposure

Visits to villages, KVKs and development agencies to observe extension delivery and farmer groups.

Communication and media work

Practicals on extension methods, audio-visual content and ICT tools; TNAU mentions media and video production within its programme.

Seminars and short internships

Seminars and, at some universities, short attachments at other institutes.

Eligibility criteria & subject prerequisites

To apply for admission to M.Sc. (Agriculture) Agricultural Extension Education, candidates must fulfill the prescribed academic qualifications and subject requirements:

  • A four-year bachelor's degree in agriculture is the standard entry qualification. For the ICAR AIEEA (PG) extension subject, recent listings have also included allied degrees such as horticulture, forestry, sericulture and agricultural marketing and co-operation; confirm in the current bulletin.
  • Minimum OGPA/percentage requirements by category are set in that year's ICAR bulletin and in each university's prospectus.
  • Final-year students can usually apply provisionally if they complete the degree with required marks by the admission deadline.
  • Domicile, reservation and seat matrices differ by state and university.

Admission routes & entrance examinations

Admissions are conducted through national, state-level and university-specific competitive selection channels:

ICAR AIEEA (PG)

National NTA-conducted test, under the Social Sciences subject group, for ICAR's share of master's seats in agricultural universities and for ICAR deemed university seats; rank also governs the ICAR PG scholarship.

University PG entrance test

Universities fill other seats through their own advertisement and entrance test. TNAU states that it screens through an entrance examination and applies EWS, community and ICAR quotas.

Step-by-step admission journey

  1. Check eligibility: Confirm your degree and OGPA against the Social Sciences group in the ICAR bulletin and the target university prospectus.
  2. Register for entrance tests: Apply for ICAR AIEEA (PG) through NTA and for the PG entrance tests of chosen universities.
  3. Attend counselling: Use your rank in ICAR online counselling or university counselling to choose the subject and institution.
  4. Complete admission: Report with original documents and fees at the allotted university within the deadline.

Fee structure & scholarships guide

Fees depend on the university and seat type. Regular seats at state agricultural universities usually cost less than self-financing seats, ICAR-quota and state-quota fees may differ, and hostel and examination charges are separate. Where extension graduates work

The degree leads to roles that connect research, government programmes and farmers, as well as research and teaching. TNAU points to work in banks, NGOs, media and policy-related analysis. Recruitment rules differ by employer and state, and selection is competitive.

Subject matter specialist (agricultural extension) at a KVK

Plans on-farm demonstrations, training and farmer outreach for a district Krishi Vigyan Kendra.

Extension or development officer

Delivers agricultural and rural development schemes through state departments or development agencies.

Programme or field coordinator in an NGO or FPO support agency

Organises farmer groups, training and project monitoring in community programmes.

Rural or agricultural banking officer

Works on agricultural lending and outreach where recruitment rules permit.

Research fellow or associate

Conducts surveys, evaluations and impact studies in university, ICAR or development research projects.

Agricultural communication or digital content specialist

Produces farmer-focused media and ICT advisory content.

Key public sector & government recruitment routes

  • ICAR ASRB National Eligibility Test (NET) in Agricultural Extension for lecturer/assistant professor eligibility
  • ICAR ASRB Agricultural Research Service and related scientist recruitment
  • State public service commission agriculture officer examinations, where the bachelor's degree qualifies

Applicant questions answered

Frequently asked questions regarding admission, course syllabus, accreditation and career prospects:

Who is eligible for M.Sc. Agricultural Extension?

Agriculture graduates are the main intake. For the ICAR AIEEA (PG) extension subject, recent listings have also admitted allied degrees such as horticulture, forestry and sericulture. Check the current bulletin and university prospectus for the exact list and minimum OGPA.

Which ICAR AIEEA (PG) group covers extension?

Agricultural Extension Education falls under the Social Sciences subject group, along with subjects such as agricultural economics.

Is this a science or a social science programme?

It is offered within the agriculture faculty but uses social-science methods. Students study behaviour, communication, institutions and evaluation, and their theses usually rely on surveys and field data from farming communities.

What does the coursework cover?

Representative BSMA areas include the extension landscape, behaviour change, organisational behaviour, research methodology, capacity development, ICTs for advisory services and impact assessment. Titles vary by university.

How much of the degree is research?

Under the ICAR BSMA framework, 30 of 70 credits are for thesis research, generally done in the second year under a major adviser.

What jobs suit extension graduates?

Common paths include KVK subject matter specialist posts, state extension and development roles, NGO and FPO programmes, rural banking, research projects and agricultural communication work.

Can I go on to a Ph.D. or teaching?

Yes. Ph.D. admission is via ICAR AICE-JRF/SRF (PhD) or university tests, and Agricultural Extension is an ASRB NET discipline for assistant professor eligibility.
