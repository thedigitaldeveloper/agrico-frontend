Postgraduate plant disease science & diagnostics degree

What an M.Sc. in Plant Pathology covers

Plant Pathology is the branch of agricultural science dedicated to understanding plant health, the etiology and biology of plant diseases, and the methods used to protect crops against devastating pathogens. From historic famines to modern rust and blast outbreaks, plant diseases present constant threats to global food systems and agricultural trade.

Conforming to ICAR's Broad Subject Matter Area (BSMA) restructured curriculum, the M.Sc. Plant Pathology degree encompasses 70 credits: 20 major credits, 8 minor credits (often chosen from Entomology, Genetics & Plant Breeding, or Microbiology), 6 supporting credits including advanced experimental statistics, 5 common research credits, 1 master's seminar credit and 30 credits dedicated to thesis research.

The curriculum provides extensive laboratory and field training across mycology (fungal taxonomy and biology), plant virology, plant bacteriology, disease physiology, molecular diagnostics (PCR, RT-PCR, ELISA), disease forecasting, chemical fungicides, and biological control agents like Trichoderma and Pseudomonas.

Normal duration 2 academic years (4 semesters)
Total credits (ICAR BSMA) 70 credits (with 30 thesis research credits)
Typical entry qualification 4-year B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture or B.Sc. (Hons.) Horticulture
National entrance examination ICAR AIEEA (PG) in the Plant Science / Plant Pathology group
Core laboratory areas Pathogen isolation, molecular detection (PCR/ELISA), fungicide bioassays and culture collections

Who should apply for this programme

This programme is well-suited for students and aspirants who align with the following academic profile and career ambitions:

  • Agriculture and horticulture graduates fascinated by plant disease symptoms, microscopic examination and microbiological lab techniques
  • Students eager to work in seed health testing, plant quarantine inspection and molecular disease diagnostics
  • Aspirants targeting scientific research careers through ICAR Agricultural Research Service (ARS) or university teaching via ASRB NET
  • Candidates interested in product development, clinical bio-efficacy trials and technical advisory in the crop protection industry

Hands-on practical learning & experiential attachments

Agricultural degrees place heavy emphasis on experiential, field-based and industrial training to ensure students graduate with job-ready operational competencies:

Pure culture isolation & pathogen identification

Isolating fungal and bacterial pathogens from infected leaf, stem and root tissues, culturing on synthetic media, and microscopic classification.

Molecular & serological diagnostics

Conducting DNA extraction, polymerase chain reaction (PCR) amplification, gel electrophoresis and ELISA assays for viral and bacterial identification.

Fungicide bioassays & poison food technique

Evaluating the efficacy of systemic and contact fungicides against fungal mycelial growth using the poisoned food technique to determine EC50 values.

Controlled pot & field disease screening

Artificial inoculation of pathogen inoculum to evaluate crop germplasm for resistance against rusts, wilts, smuts and blights.

Eligibility criteria & subject prerequisites

To apply for admission to M.Sc. (Agriculture) Plant Pathology, candidates must fulfill the prescribed academic qualifications and subject requirements:

  • Successful completion of a 4-year B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture or B.Sc. (Hons.) Horticulture from an ICAR-accredited or recognized agricultural institution.
  • Minimum required grade point average: 6.0 out of 10.0 OGPA (or 60% aggregate) for General, OBC, and EWS candidates; 5.0 out of 10.0 OGPA (or 50% aggregate) for SC, ST, and PwD candidates.
  • Final semester students awaiting results may apply provisionally, provided proof of completion is submitted at the time of final admission counselling.

Admission routes & entrance examinations

Admissions are conducted through national, state-level and university-specific competitive selection channels:

ICAR AIEEA (PG)

All-India competitive entrance exam conducted by NTA for admission to master's seats in central agricultural universities, ICAR deemed institutes, and state agricultural university quotas.

State Agricultural University PG Entrances

State exams such as UPCATET (UP), MCAER (Maharashtra), Rajasthan Pre-PG, and state university entrance portals for state domicile seats.

Private Agricultural Universities

Institutions offering ICAR-aligned curricula admit through university-level tests or merit-based screening.

Exam Name Conducting Body Key Details & Test Pattern
ICAR AIEEA (PG - Plant Science) National Testing Agency (NTA) 2-hour computer-based exam covering plant pathology, microbiology, genetics, and agricultural botany.

Step-by-step admission journey

  1. Check eligibility criteria: Review the current ICAR AIEEA (PG) Information Bulletin for qualification requirements in the Plant Science group.
  2. Register online: Submit the online application on the NTA ICAR portal and pay the examination fee before the deadline.
  3. Appear for AIEEA (PG): Take the nationwide CBT testing your grasp of plant pathology, general agriculture and allied biological sciences.
  4. Counselling & choice locking: Participate in online counselling to select your preferred university and Department of Plant Pathology based on your all-India rank.

Fee structure & scholarships guide

Private universities charge annual tuition between ₹60,000 and ₹1,40,000. Laboratory consumables and accommodation charges vary by campus.

Career pathways in research, diagnostics, regulatory and agro-industry

Postgraduates in Plant Pathology possess specialized skills essential for crop loss mitigation, seed health compliance, mushroom production, and pesticide development.

Scientist (Plant Pathology) via ARS

Lead research projects on crop disease resistance, pathogen genomics, epidemiology and climate-resilient management at ICAR research centres.

Assistant Professor / KVK Subject Matter Specialist

Educate undergraduate agricultural students and provide diagnostic field advisories to farmers facing disease outbreaks.

Plant Quarantine & Inspection Officer

Ensure consignments of imported seeds, plants and agricultural produce are free from foreign pathogens under the Plant Quarantine (Regulation of Import into India) Order.

Seed Health Analyst & Certification Officer

Conduct seed testing for seed-borne fungal, bacterial and viral pathogens in state seed certification labs and multinational seed corporations.

Fungicide Product Development Specialist

Direct bio-efficacy trials, resistance monitoring studies and registration dossiers for novel fungicidal molecules and biologicals.

Commercial Mushroom Cultivation Consultant

Design commercial spawn production facilities, compost formulations and environmental control systems for button, oyster and medicinal mushrooms.

Applicant questions answered

Frequently asked questions regarding admission, course syllabus, accreditation and career prospects:

What is the scope of M.Sc. Plant Pathology compared to M.Sc. Agronomy?

While Agronomy focuses on overall field crop production, soil management and cultural practices, Plant Pathology is a specialized branch of plant protection dedicated to disease diagnostics, microbiological analysis, fungicide development and plant quarantine compliance.

Which government exams can I take after M.Sc. Plant Pathology?

Graduates can appear for ASRB ARS Scientist and NET exams, State PSC exams for Agricultural Officer and Assistant Director (Plant Protection), SSC examinations for Plant Quarantine Inspector (DPPQS), and National Seeds Corporation (NSC) Quality Control officers.

Can I work in seed companies after doing M.Sc. in Plant Pathology?

Yes. Seed corporations recruit plant pathologists extensively for seed health testing, seed treatment formulation, screening breeding germplasm for disease resistance, and ensuring domestic and export quarantine compliance.

Is laboratory work mandatory during M.Sc. Plant Pathology?

Yes. Plant Pathology involves significant laboratory work, including pathogen isolation, pure culture maintenance, staining, microscopy, molecular testing (PCR) and fungicide bioassays alongside field experimental plots.
