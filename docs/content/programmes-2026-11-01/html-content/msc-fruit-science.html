Postgraduate horticulture degree

What the Fruit Science master's covers

M.Sc. (Horticulture) Fruit Science, sometimes called Pomology, is a specialisation within the horticulture faculty of Indian agricultural universities. It deals with the full life of a fruit crop: raising planting material in the nursery, choosing rootstocks and cultivars, planning orchards, managing canopy, nutrition and water, and handling fruit through harvest and ripening.

Most State Agricultural Universities and ICAR deemed universities follow the ICAR Broad Subject Matter Area (BSMA) PG syllabus for Horticultural Sciences, adapted by each university's academic council. The structure combines major courses in fruit science, minor and supporting courses from related departments, common courses, a seminar and a research thesis. Tamil Nadu Agricultural University, for example, describes a 70-credit load with 30 credits set aside for thesis research.

The programme is offered at many horticulture colleges. TNAU runs it at its Coimbatore horticulture college as well as Periyakulam and the Trichy college for women, and Dr YSP University of Horticulture and Forestry lists it at both Nauni and Neri. Seat numbers, crop focus and research facilities differ by region, so temperate-fruit work is more common in hill universities and tropical fruits in the plains and coastal states.

Who should apply for this programme

This programme is well-suited for students and aspirants who align with the following academic profile and career ambitions:

  • Horticulture or agriculture graduates who enjoyed orchard, nursery and propagation practicals and want to specialise in fruit crops.
  • Students interested in plant breeding, rootstocks and crop improvement for perennial fruit trees.
  • Applicants planning a research or teaching career who are ready for a year-long field or laboratory thesis.
  • Those aiming to run or advise commercial nurseries, high-density orchards or export-oriented fruit enterprises.

Hands-on practical learning & experiential attachments

Agricultural degrees place heavy emphasis on experiential, field-based and industrial training to ensure students graduate with job-ready operational competencies:

Thesis research

A supervised research project, usually begun after first-year coursework, under a major adviser and advisory committee, ending in a thesis and viva voce.

Orchard and nursery practicals

Hands-on work in university orchards and nurseries on propagation, training, pruning and crop regulation.

Seminars and short internships

Students present seminars and, at some universities, take short research attachments at other institutes.

Eligibility criteria & subject prerequisites

To apply for admission to M.Sc. (Horticulture) Fruit Science, candidates must fulfill the prescribed academic qualifications and subject requirements:

  • A four-year bachelor's degree in horticulture is the standard qualification; several universities also accept B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture for this subject. Accepted degrees vary by university and year.
  • For ICAR AIEEA (PG), candidates apply under the Horticulture subject group; the qualifying degrees and minimum OGPA/percentage for each category are notified in that year's information bulletin.
  • Final-year undergraduates can usually apply provisionally if they complete the degree with the required marks by the admission deadline.
  • University seats have their own minimum OGPA, domicile and reservation rules, which differ between states.

Admission routes & entrance examinations

Admissions are conducted through national, state-level and university-specific competitive selection channels:

ICAR AIEEA (PG)

A national NTA-conducted test used for ICAR's share of master's seats in agricultural universities and for seats at ICAR deemed universities. Rank also matters for the ICAR PG scholarship.

University PG entrance test

Most state agricultural and horticultural universities fill their remaining seats through their own entrance test or merit list. Dr YSPUHF, for instance, used its PGET score for normal and self-financing seats in the 2023 cycle.

Self-financing and other quotas

Some universities run self-financing, sports or in-service seats with separate rules; details appear in each year's prospectus.

Step-by-step admission journey

  1. Check eligibility: Confirm that your bachelor's degree and OGPA match the subject list in the ICAR bulletin and the target university's prospectus.
  2. Register for entrance tests: Apply for ICAR AIEEA (PG) through NTA and, separately, for the PG entrance of the universities you are interested in.
  3. Take part in counselling: Use your rank in ICAR online counselling or the university's own counselling to choose subject and institution.
  4. Complete admission formalities: Report to the allotted university with original documents and fees within the stated deadline.

Fee structure & scholarships guide

Fees depend on the type of institution and seat. State agricultural and horticultural universities generally charge less on regular seats than on self-financing seats, ICAR-quota and state-quota fees can differ within the same university, and hostel and examination charges are billed separately. Check the current prospectus of each university; no figures are given here because no dated official fee schedule was verified.

Where a Fruit Science master's can lead

Graduates work across public horticulture, research and the fruit value chain. Many go on to a Ph.D. or sit national eligibility tests for teaching and research posts. Job titles and recruitment rules vary by state and employer, and results depend on individual performance in competitive selection.

Horticulture officer / assistant director of horticulture

Implements state horticulture schemes, nursery programmes and grower advisory work through the state horticulture department.

Subject matter specialist (horticulture)

Delivers on-farm trials, demonstrations and training through a Krishi Vigyan Kendra.

Research fellow or associate

Supports funded projects in university or ICAR fruit research programmes.

Nursery or orchard manager

Plans propagation, planting systems and crop management for commercial nurseries and orchards.

Technical or field officer in agri-input companies

Advises growers on varieties, nutrition and crop protection for fruit crops.

Key public sector & government recruitment routes

  • ICAR ASRB National Eligibility Test (NET) in Fruit Science for lecturer/assistant professor eligibility
  • ICAR ASRB Agricultural Research Service and related scientist recruitment
  • State public service commission horticulture officer examinations

Applicant questions answered

Frequently asked questions regarding admission, course syllabus, accreditation and career prospects:

Can a B.Sc. Agriculture graduate join M.Sc. Fruit Science?

At some universities, yes. Horticulture graduates are the main intake, but several universities also accept B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture. The ICAR AIEEA (PG) bulletin and each university prospectus list the accepted degrees for that year.

Which exam is used for admission?

ICAR AIEEA (PG), conducted by NTA, covers ICAR's share of seats in agricultural universities and seats at ICAR deemed universities. Other seats are filled through each university's own PG entrance or merit process.

How is the programme structured?

Under the ICAR BSMA framework the master's carries 70 credits: 20 major, 8 minor, 6 supporting, 5 common, 1 seminar and 30 for thesis research. Coursework usually comes first, followed by the thesis.

Is Fruit Science the same as Pomology?

Yes. Pomology is the older name for the same field, and some notices still list the subject as 'Fruit Science / Pomology'.

What careers are open after the degree?

Common paths include state horticulture department posts, KVK subject matter specialist roles, research fellowships, nursery and orchard management, and technical roles in agri-input companies. Each has its own recruitment rules.

Can I teach or do research after M.Sc. Fruit Science?

Fruit Science is a discipline in the ICAR ASRB National Eligibility Test, which is used for assistant professor eligibility in agricultural universities. A Ph.D. is the usual next step for research careers.
