Postgraduate horticulture degree

What Floriculture and Landscaping involves

M.Sc. (Horticulture) Floriculture and Landscaping deals with ornamental crops and the design of green spaces. It covers cut flowers such as rose, chrysanthemum and carnation, loose flowers used in local markets, foliage and indoor plants, turf grasses, and the planning of gardens and landscapes. Production under open and protected conditions, flower seed production and value addition, including dry flowers, are also part of the field.

Universities generally base the programme on the ICAR Broad Subject Matter Area (BSMA) PG syllabus for Horticultural Sciences, with their own titles and electives. Students take major courses in floriculture and landscaping, minor and supporting courses from related departments, common courses and a seminar, and complete a thesis. TNAU describes a 70-credit load with 30 credits for thesis research and says its programme follows ICAR guidelines.

Naming differs: the ICAR Horticulture group lists 'Floriculture and Landscaping Architecture', TNAU and Dr YSPUHF advertise 'Floriculture and Landscaping', and some university departments use 'Landscape Architecture'. TNAU offers the programme at Coimbatore and Periyakulam, and Dr YSPUHF at Nauni and Neri.

Who should apply for this programme

This programme is well-suited for students and aspirants who align with the following academic profile and career ambitions:

  • Horticulture graduates who enjoyed ornamental gardening, nursery and flower crop practicals.
  • Students with an eye for design who want to apply plant knowledge to gardens, campuses and urban green spaces.
  • Applicants interested in protected cultivation of cut flowers or in flower seed and planting-material production.
  • Those planning research or teaching careers, or a nursery, landscaping or floral value-addition business.

Hands-on practical learning & experiential attachments

Agricultural degrees place heavy emphasis on experiential, field-based and industrial training to ensure students graduate with job-ready operational competencies:

Thesis research

A supervised project on a flower crop, ornamental plant or landscape topic, usually begun after first-year coursework and defended at a final viva voce.

Protected structures and nursery work

Practicals in polyhouses, shade nets and ornamental nurseries on propagation, crop scheduling and quality management.

Landscape design exercises

Planning garden and landscape layouts, increasingly with design software where the course includes CAD.

Study tours and seminars

Visits to flower farms, gardens and research stations, plus seminars, at universities that schedule them.

Eligibility criteria & subject prerequisites

To apply for admission to M.Sc. (Horticulture) Floriculture and Landscaping, candidates must fulfill the prescribed academic qualifications and subject requirements:

  • A four-year bachelor's degree in horticulture is the usual qualification; some universities also accept B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture. Accepted degrees vary by university and year.
  • For ICAR AIEEA (PG), the subject is in the Horticulture group; qualifying degrees and minimum OGPA/percentage by category are notified in that year's bulletin.
  • Final-year students can usually apply provisionally if they complete the degree with required marks by the admission deadline.
  • University seats carry their own OGPA, domicile and reservation rules, which differ by state.

Admission routes & entrance examinations

Admissions are conducted through national, state-level and university-specific competitive selection channels:

ICAR AIEEA (PG)

National NTA-conducted test for ICAR's share of master's seats in agricultural universities and for ICAR deemed university seats; rank also decides ICAR PG scholarship eligibility.

University PG entrance test

Universities fill their other seats through their own entrance tests or merit lists; Dr YSPUHF, for example, used PGET scores in the 2023 cycle.

Self-financing and other quotas

Some universities run self-financing, sports or in-service seats under separate rules in the prospectus.

Step-by-step admission journey

  1. Check eligibility: Compare your degree and OGPA with the ICAR bulletin and target university prospectuses, noting the exact programme title each uses.
  2. Register for entrance tests: Apply for ICAR AIEEA (PG) through NTA and for chosen universities' PG entrance tests.
  3. Attend counselling: Use your rank in ICAR online counselling or university counselling to pick the subject and institution.
  4. Complete admission: Report with original documents and fees at the allotted university within the deadline.

Fee structure & scholarships guide

Fees differ between universities and seat categories. Regular seats at state agricultural and horticultural universities are usually cheaper than self-financing seats, ICAR-quota and state-quota fees may differ, and hostel and exam charges are separate. Careers in flowers, nurseries and landscapes

Graduates find work in public horticulture, research and the ornamental plant industry, and some set up their own nursery, landscaping or floral businesses. A Ph.D. or national eligibility test opens teaching and research routes. Recruitment rules vary by employer and state, and selection is competitive.

Horticulture officer

Implements state horticulture schemes, including floriculture and nursery programmes, through the state department.

Subject matter specialist (horticulture) at a KVK

Runs demonstrations and training for growers, including flower crop production.

Landscape horticulturist or garden supervisor

Plans and maintains gardens, campuses, parks and turf areas for public bodies or private firms.

Nursery or flower farm manager

Manages propagation, crop scheduling and quality for ornamental nurseries and cut-flower units.

Research fellow or associate

Supports funded floriculture research in universities and ICAR institutes.

Key public sector & government recruitment routes

  • ICAR ASRB National Eligibility Test (NET) in Floriculture and Landscaping for lecturer/assistant professor eligibility
  • ICAR ASRB Agricultural Research Service and related scientist recruitment
  • State public service commission horticulture officer examinations

Applicant questions answered

Frequently asked questions regarding admission, course syllabus, accreditation and career prospects:

Who can apply for M.Sc. Floriculture and Landscaping?

Mainly horticulture graduates; some universities also accept B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture. The ICAR AIEEA (PG) bulletin and each prospectus list accepted degrees and minimum OGPA.

Is it the same as Floriculture and Landscape Architecture?

Broadly yes. The ICAR Horticulture group uses 'Floriculture and Landscaping Architecture', while universities use either 'Floriculture and Landscaping' or 'Landscape Architecture' in the title. Check the exact name in each university's list.

Which entrance exams apply?

ICAR AIEEA (PG), conducted by NTA, for ICAR-quota and ICAR deemed university seats, and university-level PG entrance tests or merit lists for other seats.

Does the course include landscape design?

Yes. Ornamental gardening and landscaping is a core area, and the BSMA course areas also cover turf management, interiorscaping and computer-aided landscape design. Coverage depends on the university.

How much research is involved?

Thesis research carries 30 of the 70 credits in the ICAR BSMA framework and is usually done in the second year under a major adviser.

What careers can I pursue?

Options include state horticulture posts, KVK roles, landscaping and nursery management, cut-flower farm management, research fellowships and running a floriculture business.

Can I go on to a Ph.D. or teaching?

Yes. Ph.D. admission is through ICAR AICE-JRF/SRF (PhD) or university tests, and Floriculture and Landscaping is listed as an ASRB NET discipline for assistant professor eligibility.
