Postgraduate farm economics, finance & policy degree

What an M.Sc. in Agricultural Economics covers

Agricultural Economics is an applied branch of economics that examines the allocation of scarce natural and economic resources in agricultural production, farm management, food processing, marketing, and international commodity trade. In an era marked by fluctuating farm gate prices, supply chain friction, climate vulnerability and evolving agricultural policies, agricultural economists provide critical analytical insights for sustainable policymaking and agribusiness investment.

In accordance with ICAR's Broad Subject Matter Area (BSMA) restructured curriculum, the two-year M.Sc. Agricultural Economics programme comprises 70 total credits: 20 major credits, 8 minor credits (often chosen from Agricultural Extension, Statistics, or Agronomy), 6 supporting credits in statistical tools and mathematics, 5 common research credits, 1 master's seminar credit and 30 credits dedicated to thesis research.

The curriculum covers advanced microeconomic theory, macroeconomic policy, agricultural production economics, farm business management, econometric modelling using software packages (STATA, R, SPSS), agricultural marketing and price analysis, rural finance and microcredit, natural resource and environmental economics, and international trade regulations under the WTO.

Who should apply for this programme

This programme is well-suited for students and aspirants who align with the following academic profile and career ambitions:

  • Agriculture and allied graduates who have a strong aptitude for mathematics, statistics, data analytics and economic thinking
  • Students keen on understanding agricultural market dynamics, MSP (Minimum Support Price) mechanisms, food inflation and supply chain efficiency
  • Aspirants targeting national policy and research careers in organizations such as NITI Aayog, NABARD, RBI, ICAR-NIAP or international bodies like IFPRI and the World Bank
  • Candidates aiming for banking specialist officer cadres (IBPS SO / NABARD Grade A) or commercial commodity market analyst roles

Hands-on practical learning & experiential attachments

Agricultural degrees place heavy emphasis on experiential, field-based and industrial training to ensure students graduate with job-ready operational competencies:

Farm survey design & field data collection

Designing structured questionnaires, conducting primary farmer interviews in rural villages, and collecting farm budget, input-cost and yield data.

Econometric & regression analysis

Using software packages (R, STATA, SPSS) to run multiple regression, Cobb-Douglas production functions, time-series forecasting and price volatility analysis.

Market yard (APMC Mandi) studies

Observing electronic auction systems (e-NAM), recording mandi arrivals, calculating price spreads and assessing marketing margins across intermediary channels.

Supervised master's research thesis

Independent empirical research on an agricultural economics problem—such as crop insurance uptake, groundwater economics, value chain analysis or impact assessment of government schemes.

Eligibility criteria & subject prerequisites

To apply for admission to M.Sc. (Agriculture) Agricultural Economics, candidates must fulfill the prescribed academic qualifications and subject requirements:

  • Completion of a 4-year B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture, B.Sc. (Hons.) Horticulture, B.Sc. (Hons.) Forestry, or B.Sc. Agribusiness Management from an ICAR-accredited or recognized agricultural institution.
  • Minimum required grade point average: 6.0/10.0 OGPA (or 60% aggregate) for General, OBC and EWS candidates; 5.0/10.0 OGPA (or 50% aggregate) for SC, ST and PwD candidates.
  • Final semester students may apply provisionally, provided they submit qualifying marksheets at the time of final admission counselling.

Admission routes & entrance examinations

Admissions are conducted through national, state-level and university-specific competitive selection channels:

ICAR AIEEA (PG - Social Sciences)

National entrance examination conducted by NTA for all-India quota seats in State Agricultural Universities, Central Universities, and ICAR deemed institutes (such as IARI New Delhi and NDRI Karnal).

State Agricultural University PG Entrances

Conducted by state bodies such as UPCATET (UP), MCAER (Maharashtra), OUAT (Odisha), Rajasthan Pre-PG, and state agricultural university portals.

Private Agricultural Universities

Admissions conducted through university-level tests or merit screening aligned with ICAR standards.

Exam Name Conducting Body Key Details & Test Pattern
ICAR AIEEA (PG - Social Sciences) National Testing Agency (NTA) Computer-based exam testing Agricultural Economics, Agricultural Extension, Agricultural Statistics, and general agriculture fundamentals.

Step-by-step admission journey

  1. Check Social Sciences eligibility: Verify your degree eligibility under the Social Sciences / Agricultural Economics subject group in the ICAR AIEEA (PG) Information Bulletin.
  2. Complete online application: Register on the NTA ICAR portal, upload documents and select examination centre preferences.
  3. Appear for entrance examination: Take the 120-question, 2-hour CBT assessing economic principles, farm management, statistics and current rural developments.
  4. Participate in online counselling: Select participating agricultural universities and departments of Agricultural Economics based on merit rank.

Fee structure & scholarships guide

State Agricultural Universities and ICAR institutes provide highly subsidised education with annual tuition fees typically ranging between ₹14,000 and ₹30,000 for merit quota seats. Candidates admitted via ICAR AIEEA (PG) with top ranks receive the ICAR PG Scholarship / National Talent Scholarship with monthly financial allowances. Private agricultural universities charge between ₹60,000 and ₹1,35,000 per academic year. Computer lab and accommodation charges are billed as per actual university rules.

Professional roles in banking, economic research, policy and commodities

Agricultural Economics postgraduates possess unique quantitative and socio-economic skills, making them highly versatile across public policy institutions, financial banks, research think-tanks and commodity corporations.

Scientist (Agricultural Economics) via ARS

Lead socio-economic impact studies, farm income assessments, technology adoption evaluations and policy analyses at ICAR research institutes (NIAP, IARI, CRIDA).

Assistant Manager / Economist at NABARD & RBI

Appraise rural credit schemes, assess Priority Sector Lending (PSL) performance, analyze rural credit flow and formulate state potential-linked credit plans (PLP).

Agricultural Field Officer / Credit Analyst in Commercial Banks

Appraise agricultural loan portfolios, evaluate agro-processing project feasibility reports, and monitor farm credit recovery across regional rural branches.

Commodity Research Analyst

Analyze crop acreage forecasts, domestic arrivals, export-import trends and price volatility for commodity exchanges (NCDEX, MCX), brokerage houses and agribusiness conglomerates.

Assistant Professor / KVK Subject Matter Specialist

Teach undergraduate and master's courses in agricultural economics and farm management, and conduct village surveys for technology adoption.

Policy Researcher at Think Tanks (IFPRI / RIS / NITI Aayog)

Conduct quantitative economic research on food security, crop insurance schemes (PMFBY), irrigation investments and rural labour transitions.

Applicant questions answered

Frequently asked questions regarding admission, course syllabus, accreditation and career prospects:

What is the difference between an M.Sc. in Agricultural Economics and an MBA in Agribusiness?

M.Sc. Agricultural Economics is an analytical, research-oriented degree emphasizing micro and macroeconomic theory, econometrics, farm management, price policy, rural finance, and socio-economic research. An MBA in Agribusiness focuses more on corporate business operations, sales & marketing, HR, logistics, supply chain management, and enterprise leadership.

Are Agricultural Economics graduates eligible for banking jobs?

Yes. Agricultural Economics postgraduates are highly preferred candidates for NABARD Grade A Officer posts, RBI Research posts, and IBPS Specialist Officer (Agricultural Field Officer) roles in public sector banks.

What kind of software do students learn in M.Sc. Agricultural Economics?

Students typically receive hands-on training in statistical and econometric software packages such as STATA, R, SPSS, SAS, and advanced spreadsheet tools for regression analysis, linear programming and data visualization.

Is ASRB NET required for teaching Agricultural Economics?

Yes. Passing the Agricultural Scientists Recruitment Board (ASRB) National Eligibility Test (NET) in Agricultural Economics is the standard requirement for appointment as an Assistant Professor or KVK Subject Matter Specialist across State Agricultural Universities.
