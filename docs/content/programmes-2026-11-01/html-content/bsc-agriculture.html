Undergraduate agriculture degree

What the B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture degree covers

B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture is the core professional degree of India's agricultural university system. Most programmes follow the course structure recommended by ICAR's Deans Committees. The Sixth Deans Committee report, published in volumes on ICAR's e-book portal, restructured the undergraduate curricula to fit the National Education Policy 2020. That means more skill-enhancement courses, credit transfer and defined exit points.

The programme combines plant, animal and social sciences. Students study agronomy, soil science, plant breeding, entomology, plant pathology, horticulture, agricultural economics and extension education. Lectures run alongside farm work and laboratory practicals. Tamil Nadu Agricultural University (TNAU), for example, describes its version as four years of coursework with farm and laboratory exposure, industry internships and experiential learning courses run on an earn-while-you-learn basis.

The degree is offered by state agricultural universities, central agricultural universities, ICAR deemed universities, and general and private universities with agriculture faculties. Seats, admission rules, fees and course titles depend on the institution. Applicants should check each university's current prospectus and ICAR accreditation status before applying.

Who should apply for this programme

This programme is well-suited for students and aspirants who align with the following academic profile and career ambitions:

  • Students who studied biology, agriculture or mathematics in Class 12 alongside physics and chemistry and want to apply science to food production
  • Applicants who are comfortable with outdoor field work, seasonal farm schedules and laboratory practicals
  • Students interested in rural development, farmer advisory work or extension communication
  • Those planning a career in agri-input, seed or food businesses, or aiming for agricultural research through postgraduate study
  • Aspiring agri-entrepreneurs who want structured exposure to farm enterprises before starting their own

Hands-on practical learning & experiential attachments

Agricultural degrees place heavy emphasis on experiential, field-based and industrial training to ensure students graduate with job-ready operational competencies:

Rural Agricultural Work Experience (RAWE)

Students are attached to villages and partner agencies to study farming systems, diagnose field problems and take part in extension activities with farmers.

Experiential Learning Programme (ELP)

Small teams run a production or service unit, such as seed production, biofertiliser or biocontrol inputs, protected cultivation or value addition, from planning through to sale.

Industry / institutional attachment

Short placements with agri-input, seed, processing or research organisations to see how work is done outside the campus.

Farm and laboratory practicals

Regular field practicals on instructional farms and laboratory classes run alongside the theory courses from the first year.

Eligibility criteria & subject prerequisites

To apply for admission to B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture, candidates must fulfill the prescribed academic qualifications and subject requirements:

  • Class 12 (10+2) or equivalent from a recognised board is the base requirement for entry to the four-year degree.
  • For ICAR-quota seats through CUET-UG (2025 cycle), candidates needed at least three of Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics, Biology and Agriculture in Class 12, and had to take the same subjects in CUET.
  • The ICAR-quota minimum aggregate in Class 12 was 50% for General, OBC-NCL, EWS and UPS candidates, and 40% for SC, ST, PwBD and third-gender candidates, with no rounding off (2025 cycle; confirm in the current bulletin).
  • ICAR-quota candidates had to be at least 16 years old on the cut-off date stated in the bulletin (31 August of the admission year in the 2025 cycle).
  • State-quota seats follow state rules. Subject combinations, domicile requirements, reservation and minimum marks vary by state and university. Some states also run separate entry routes for agriculture diploma holders.

Admission routes & entrance examinations

Admissions are conducted through national, state-level and university-specific competitive selection channels:

CUET-UG (ICAR quota seats)

ICAR uses CUET-UG scores, with the test run by NTA, to fill its all-India quota through online counselling. In the 2025 cycle this covered 20% of seats in participating state agricultural universities and all seats in ICAR deemed universities and central agricultural universities.

State agricultural entrance / counselling

Most remaining seats go through state processes. Depending on the state, this means a state entrance test, a common engineering-and-agriculture test, or merit in Class 12 marks. For example, ANGRAU (Andhra Pradesh) published 2026 counselling for B.Sc. (Hons) Agriculture using AP EAPCET ranks and, separately, AP AGRICET ranks.

University entrance or merit

Private and deemed universities with agriculture faculties usually admit through their own entrance test or qualifying-exam merit. Check whether the programme is ICAR-accredited before applying.

Step-by-step admission journey

  1. Shortlist routes: Decide whether you are applying for ICAR quota seats, your home-state seats, private universities, or more than one.
  2. Match CUET subjects to Class 12: If you are taking CUET-UG, choose domain subjects that match the core subjects on your Class 12 marksheet.
  3. Register for counselling: After results, register separately for ICAR counselling and any state counselling, fill in your choices, and track the round schedules.
  4. Verify documents and report: Complete document verification, pay the seat acceptance or admission fee within the deadline, and report to the allotted college.

Fee structure & scholarships guide

Fees differ widely. State agricultural universities generally charge government-regulated fees, which still differ between states and between government and self-financing seats. Private and deemed universities set their own fees, and these are often much higher. Hostel, mess, RAWE travel and examination charges may be billed separately. Use the current official fee notice of each institution.

Where agriculture graduates work

Graduates work in farm advisory, input supply, banking, research support and food businesses, and some start their own ventures. Government jobs are filled through competitive recruitment, and postgraduate study opens research and teaching roles. Outcomes depend on the individual, the institution and recruitment cycles.

Agriculture officer / extension officer

Works with state agriculture departments to deliver schemes, advisories and demonstrations to farmers.

Agricultural field officer (banking)

Assesses and monitors farm and rural loans for banks.

Agri-input sales and technical executive

Supports dealers and farmers on seeds, fertilisers and crop-protection products.

Seed production and quality staff

Organises seed multiplication, field inspection and quality testing for seed companies or agencies.

Agri-clinic or agri-business entrepreneur

Runs an advisory, input, nursery or value-addition enterprise.

Key public sector & government recruitment routes

  • IBPS Specialist Officer – Agricultural Field Officer
  • State public service commission agriculture officer examinations
  • ICAR AIEEA (PG) for postgraduate admission and fellowships
  • ASRB NET after a relevant master's degree (for teaching eligibility)

Applicant questions answered

Frequently asked questions regarding admission, course syllabus, accreditation and career prospects:

Can I apply with PCM instead of PCB?

Under the ICAR-quota CUET route in the 2025 cycle, any three of Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics, Biology and Agriculture could qualify, so PCM was accepted, provided you took the same subjects in CUET. State and private universities may set different subject rules, so check each prospectus.

Is CUET compulsory for B.Sc. Agriculture?

Only for ICAR all-India quota seats and for institutions that fill seats through CUET. Most state-quota seats are filled through state entrance tests or state merit counselling, and private universities run their own processes.

What is RAWE?

Rural Agricultural Work Experience is the village-attachment part of the final-year Student READY programme. Students work with farm families and local agencies to understand field conditions and support extension activities.

Is B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture the same as a three-year B.Sc. in agriculture?

No. The ICAR-model degree is a four-year professional programme. Some public-sector notifications, such as banking agricultural field officer posts, ask for a four-year degree, so check the duration and accreditation of any programme you are considering.

What can I study after B.Sc. Agriculture?

Common options are an M.Sc. (Agriculture) in a specialised discipline, admission to which ICAR's national PG entrance and university tests support, or an MBA or PG diploma in agribusiness. A Ph.D. can follow later.

Do all universities follow the same syllabus?

Most agricultural universities base their syllabus on the ICAR Deans Committee recommendations, but they adapt course titles, credits and timing. Your university's academic regulations are the final reference.
