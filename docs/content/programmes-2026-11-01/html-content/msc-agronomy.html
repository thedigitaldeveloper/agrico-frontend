Postgraduate crop production degree

What an M.Sc. in Agronomy covers

Agronomy is the branch of agricultural science that deals with producing crops efficiently on a given piece of land: choosing crops and cropping systems, managing nutrients and irrigation, controlling weeds and conserving soil. At master's level in India, the programme is normally offered by state agricultural universities, ICAR deemed universities and central agricultural universities, as well as some private universities that follow the ICAR syllabus.

Under ICAR's Broad Subject Matter Area (BSMA) restructured curriculum, a master's student completes 70 credits: 20 in major (agronomy) courses, 8 in a minor subject, 6 in supporting courses such as statistics, 5 in common courses, a 1-credit seminar and 30 credits of thesis research. Core agronomy courses include modern concepts in crop production, soil fertility and nutrient management, weed management and water management.

The revised syllabus also adds newer topics such as conservation agriculture, precision farming, protected and soilless cultivation, and the use of GIS, GPS and remote sensing. Course codes, elective menus and the minor subjects available differ from one university to another.

Who should apply for this programme

This programme is well-suited for students and aspirants who align with the following academic profile and career ambitions:

  • Agriculture graduates who enjoyed field crop production, farming systems and on-farm experiments during the undergraduate programme
  • Students comfortable spending long periods on research farms, recording crop observations across a season
  • Applicants interested in water, nutrient and weed management problems faced by farmers in their region
  • Those planning a research, teaching or extension career, or a Ph.D. in crop science
  • Learners willing to use statistics and data tools to design and analyse experiments

Course structure under the ICAR restructured syllabus

The programme follows ICAR's credit framework: core and elective agronomy courses, a minor from a related department, supporting statistics courses, common research-skills courses and a thesis. Course titles below come from a university syllabus prepared as per BSMA; subject names, codes and electives vary by university, so check your admitting university's course catalogue.

Hands-on practical learning & experiential attachments

Agricultural degrees place heavy emphasis on experiential, field-based and industrial training to ensure students graduate with job-ready operational competencies:

Thesis research

Each student plans and runs an experiment, often on the university research farm, under an advisor and advisory committee, then writes and defends a thesis.

Practical classes

Most agronomy courses carry practical credits covering field layout, nutrient and water budgeting, weed surveys and crop growth measurements.

Master's seminar

A one-credit seminar in which the student reviews recent literature on a topic and presents it to the department.

Eligibility criteria & subject prerequisites

To apply for admission to M.Sc. (Agriculture) Agronomy, candidates must fulfill the prescribed academic qualifications and subject requirements:

  • A bachelor's degree in agriculture, normally the four-year B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture; some universities and the ICAR bulletin also accept certain allied degrees for agronomy, so check the subject-wise eligibility list for the admission year.
  • A minimum overall grade point average or percentage in the bachelor's degree, with relaxation for reserved categories; the threshold is set in the ICAR AIEEA (PG) bulletin and in each university's prospectus.
  • For ICAR quota seats, the bachelor's degree is generally expected to be from an institution and programme that meet ICAR's norms; requirements vary, so confirm in the current bulletin.
  • Final-year students can usually apply provisionally but must show the completed degree by the date the admitting university specifies.

Admission routes & entrance examinations

Admissions are conducted through national, state-level and university-specific competitive selection channels:

ICAR AIEEA (PG)

A national entrance test conducted by NTA on behalf of ICAR. Its merit list is used for ICAR's all-India quota seats in agricultural universities and for ICAR PG scholarships. Candidates choose a major subject group; Agronomy has its own group.

State agricultural university entrance or merit

Seats outside the ICAR quota in state agricultural universities are filled through the university's or state's own entrance test, interview or merit process, which differs by state.

Private and other universities

Private universities that follow the ICAR syllabus usually run their own entrance test or admit on merit; procedures and seat numbers are set by each institution.

Exam Name Conducting Body Key Details & Test Pattern
ICAR AIEEA (PG) National Testing Agency (NTA) for ICAR Computer-based multiple-choice test in the chosen subject group. Secondary reports of recent cycles describe 120 questions in 2 hours with negative marking; confirm the pattern in that year's information bulletin.

Step-by-step admission journey

  1. Check subject-wise eligibility: Read the current ICAR AIEEA (PG) bulletin and your target universities' prospectuses to confirm that your bachelor's degree qualifies for agronomy.
  2. Register for the entrance: Apply online for ICAR AIEEA (PG) under the Agronomy subject group and for any state or university entrance you also intend to take.
  3. Take the test and follow counselling: After results, register for ICAR online counselling or the relevant university counselling and fill in university and subject choices.
  4. Report for admission: Complete document verification and fee payment at the allotted university within the notified dates.

Fee structure & scholarships guide

Fees for M.Sc. Agronomy vary widely. State agricultural universities usually charge much less than private universities, and some universities charge different amounts to ICAR quota, state quota, out-of-state and NRI or management seats. Hostel and examination charges are often billed separately. Students admitted through ICAR AIEEA (PG) may be eligible for an ICAR PG scholarship under that year's rules. Check the current fee notice of the admitting university.

Where an agronomy master's can lead

Agronomy postgraduates work wherever crop production decisions are made: in research stations, extension systems, input companies and farm advisory services. Many continue to a Ph.D. Government routes usually need separate competitive exams, so the degree is a qualification for applying rather than a guarantee of selection.

Research associate or senior research fellow

Supports field trials on crop varieties, nutrients, irrigation or weed control in ICAR institutes, universities or funded projects.

Agronomist in agri-input or seed companies

Plans demonstration plots and advises farmers and dealers on crop management for the company's products.

Subject matter specialist (agronomy)

Delivers crop production training and on-farm trials, for example at Krishi Vigyan Kendras, subject to recruitment rules.

Farm or plantation manager

Manages crop planning, input use and labour on large farms, estates or contract-farming operations.

Assistant professor or scientist (with further qualifications)

Teaching and research posts generally also require qualifying ASRB or UGC NET and often a Ph.D.

Key public sector & government recruitment routes

  • ASRB NET (Agronomy) for lecturer or assistant professor eligibility in agricultural universities
  • ASRB Agricultural Research Service (ARS) examination for scientist posts in ICAR
  • State public service commission agriculture officer recruitment
  • IBPS Agricultural Field Officer (Specialist Officer) recruitment

Applicant questions answered

Frequently asked questions regarding admission, course syllabus, accreditation and career prospects:

Who is eligible for M.Sc. Agronomy?

Normally graduates of the four-year B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture. Some universities and the ICAR bulletin list additional allied degrees, and a minimum grade point average applies, so check the current subject-wise eligibility rules.

Which entrance exam do I need?

ICAR AIEEA (PG), run by NTA, is used for ICAR quota seats and ICAR PG scholarships. Other seats are filled through state or university entrance tests or merit, depending on the institution.

How long is the course and how is it structured?

Two years over four semesters. Under the ICAR framework, students earn 70 credits: 40 from major, minor, supporting and common courses plus a seminar, and 30 from thesis research.

Is a thesis compulsory?

Yes. Thesis research carries 30 of the 70 credits in the ICAR framework, and the student must submit and defend a thesis before the degree is awarded.

What jobs can I apply for after M.Sc. Agronomy?

Research project positions, agronomist roles in seed and input companies, farm management and extension roles, and government posts through competitive exams such as state agriculture officer or IBPS AFO recruitment.

Can I do a Ph.D. after this degree?

Yes. Ph.D. Agronomy admissions in agricultural universities use ICAR AICE-JRF/SRF (Ph.D.) for ICAR seats and fellowships, along with university-level processes.
