Postgraduate crop improvement degree

Studying genetics and plant breeding at master's level

Genetics and Plant Breeding (often shortened to GPB) is the discipline behind new crop varieties and hybrids. It explains how traits such as yield, quality and resistance to pests, diseases or drought are inherited, and uses that knowledge to select and combine better plant types. In India, the M.Sc. is offered by state agricultural universities, ICAR deemed universities, central agricultural universities and private universities that follow the ICAR syllabus.

ICAR's restructured postgraduate framework requires 70 credits for a master's degree: 20 major, 8 minor, 6 supporting, 5 common, a 1-credit seminar and 30 credits of thesis research. Compulsory major courses in published GPB syllabi include principles of genetics, cytogenetics, plant breeding and quantitative genetics, along with cell biology and molecular genetics and biotechnology for crop improvement.

Electives typically cover mutation and heterosis breeding, breeding for stress resistance and quality, crop-group breeding, maintenance breeding and seed production, and plant genetic resources. The exact list and course codes depend on the university.

Who should apply for this programme

This programme is well-suited for students and aspirants who align with the following academic profile and career ambitions:

  • Agriculture graduates who liked genetics, cytology and crop improvement courses at undergraduate level
  • Students patient enough for season-long work making crosses, raising generations and scoring plants
  • Applicants curious about molecular markers, biotechnology and how they speed up variety development
  • Those aiming for careers in seed companies, breeding programmes or crop research institutes
  • Learners comfortable with the statistics used to estimate heritability and genetic variance

Course structure under the ICAR framework

Students take compulsory and elective GPB courses, a minor from a related plant science, supporting statistics or biotechnology courses, common research-skills courses and a thesis. The titles below come from published university syllabi aligned with ICAR BSMA; subject names, codes and electives vary by university.

Hands-on practical learning & experiential attachments

Agricultural degrees place heavy emphasis on experiential, field-based and industrial training to ensure students graduate with job-ready operational competencies:

Thesis research

Students work on a breeding or genetics problem, such as evaluating germplasm, studying inheritance of a trait or testing hybrids, and defend a thesis at the end.

Field and laboratory practicals

Practical sessions cover emasculation and crossing, maintaining breeding records, chromosome preparation and basic molecular techniques.

Master's seminar

A one-credit seminar reviewing recent research on a chosen topic in crop improvement.

Eligibility criteria & subject prerequisites

To apply for admission to M.Sc. (Agriculture) Genetics and Plant Breeding, candidates must fulfill the prescribed academic qualifications and subject requirements:

  • A four-year bachelor's degree in agriculture, typically B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture; the ICAR bulletin and some universities also accept certain allied degrees for this subject, so check the subject-wise eligibility list for the admission year.
  • A minimum overall grade point average or percentage in the bachelor's degree, with relaxation for reserved categories, as set in the ICAR AIEEA (PG) bulletin and each university's prospectus.
  • Requirements on the recognition or accreditation of the bachelor's degree vary; confirm them in the current bulletin.
  • Final-year students can usually apply but must complete the degree by the date the admitting university specifies.

Admission routes & entrance examinations

Admissions are conducted through national, state-level and university-specific competitive selection channels:

ICAR AIEEA (PG)

National entrance test by NTA for ICAR. Genetics and Plant Breeding is one of the subjects under the Plant Sciences major subject group. The merit list is used for ICAR all-India quota seats and ICAR PG scholarships.

State agricultural university entrance or merit

Remaining seats in state agricultural universities are filled through state or university entrance tests, interviews or merit lists, which differ by state.

Private and other universities

Private universities following the ICAR syllabus generally hold their own entrance or merit-based admissions.

Exam Name Conducting Body Key Details & Test Pattern
ICAR AIEEA (PG) National Testing Agency (NTA) for ICAR Candidates appear in the Plant Sciences subject group. Secondary reports describe a 120-question, 2-hour computer-based test with negative marking; confirm in the current bulletin.

Step-by-step admission journey

  1. Confirm eligibility: Check the subject-wise eligibility annexure of the ICAR AIEEA (PG) bulletin and target university prospectuses for Genetics and Plant Breeding.
  2. Apply for entrance tests: Register for ICAR AIEEA (PG) in the Plant Sciences group and for any state or university tests you plan to take.
  3. Participate in counselling: After results, register for ICAR or university counselling and list your preferred universities and subjects.
  4. Complete admission: Attend document verification and pay fees at the allotted university within the notified schedule.

Fee structure & scholarships guide

Fees differ sharply between state agricultural universities, ICAR deemed universities and private universities, and between ICAR quota, state quota and management or NRI seats where these exist. Hostel and examination charges are often extra. ICAR AIEEA (PG) qualifiers admitted on ICAR seats may receive an ICAR PG scholarship under that year's rules. Always check the admitting university's current fee notice.

Career directions in crop improvement

Plant breeding postgraduates are recruited by public breeding programmes, seed companies and biotechnology firms, and many go on to doctoral research. Public sector roles are filled through competitive examinations, so the degree provides eligibility rather than assured placement.

Assistant breeder or breeding associate

Manages nurseries, crossing blocks and yield trials within a seed company or public breeding programme.

Research fellow in crop improvement projects

Works on funded projects in ICAR institutes or universities, collecting phenotypic and genotypic data.

Seed production and quality officer

Supervises seed multiplication, maintains varietal purity and coordinates with certification agencies.

Germplasm or genebank assistant

Helps characterise, document and conserve plant genetic resources.

Assistant professor or scientist (with further qualifications)

Usually requires qualifying ASRB or UGC NET and, increasingly, a Ph.D.

Key public sector & government recruitment routes

  • ASRB NET (Genetics and Plant Breeding)
  • ASRB Agricultural Research Service (ARS) examination for ICAR scientist posts
  • State public service commission agriculture officer recruitment
  • IBPS Agricultural Field Officer (Specialist Officer) recruitment

Applicant questions answered

Frequently asked questions regarding admission, course syllabus, accreditation and career prospects:

Who can apply for M.Sc. Genetics and Plant Breeding?

Mainly B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture graduates, with some allied degrees accepted by certain universities and in the ICAR bulletin. A minimum grade point average applies; check the subject-wise eligibility for the admission year.

Which ICAR AIEEA (PG) subject group covers this course?

Genetics and Plant Breeding falls under the Plant Sciences major subject group of ICAR AIEEA (PG).

What are the core subjects?

Published BSMA-based syllabi list principles of genetics, cytogenetics, plant breeding and quantitative genetics, together with cell biology and molecular genetics and biotechnology for crop improvement, as compulsory courses.

How much of the degree is research?

In ICAR's restructured framework, thesis research carries 30 of the 70 credits, and the student must submit and defend a thesis.

Is this course only about molecular biology?

No. Molecular tools are part of the syllabus, but much of the work involves field breeding: making crosses, evaluating populations and running trials over crop seasons.

What can I do after the M.Sc.?

Options include breeding and seed roles in industry, research project posts, competitive exams such as ASRB NET or ARS, and a Ph.D. through ICAR AICE-JRF/SRF or university entrance.
