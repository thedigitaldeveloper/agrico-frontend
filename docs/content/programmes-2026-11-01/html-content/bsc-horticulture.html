Undergraduate horticulture degree

What the B.Sc. (Hons.) Horticulture degree covers

B.Sc. (Hons.) Horticulture is a four-year professional undergraduate degree designed under the recommendations of the ICAR Deans Committee. The programme equips students with scientific knowledge and technical skills in fruit science (pomology), vegetable science (olericulture), floriculture, landscape gardening, plantation crops, spices, aromatic and medicinal plants, and post-harvest technology.

The curriculum balances plant biology, soil management, plant protection, and breeding with hands-on production technologies such as greenhouse cultivation, drip irrigation, micropropagation, nursery management, and hydroponics. Students gain extensive field exposure on university orchards, commercial nurseries, and processing units.

In the final year, students undergo Rural Horticultural Work Experience (RHWE) and the Experiential Learning Programme (ELP) under the Student READY scheme, operating commercial enterprise units such as commercial floriculture, nursery production, or fruit and vegetable processing on an earn-while-you-learn basis.

Who should apply for this programme

This programme is well-suited for students and aspirants who align with the following academic profile and career ambitions:

  • Students with Physics, Chemistry, Biology/Mathematics or Agriculture in Class 12 interested in plant production, genetics, and horticultural sciences
  • Those enthusiastic about orchard management, landscape design, greenhouse farming, and ornamental plant production
  • Applicants aspiring to establish commercial enterprises in protected cultivation, seed production, nursery management, or floriculture export
  • Students aiming for government roles as Horticulture Development Officers or for scientific careers in ICAR research institutes and universities

How the four years are organised

The course structure follows the national curriculum formulated by the ICAR Deans Committee. While individual state agricultural and horticultural universities structure courses by semester, the core domains remain consistent across accredited institutions.

Stage 1

Years 1-2: Scientific Foundations & Basic Horticulture

Basic sciences, fundamentals of horticulture, soil fertility, plant anatomy, introductory genetics, and crop protection.

Hands-on practical learning & experiential attachments

Agricultural degrees place heavy emphasis on experiential, field-based and industrial training to ensure students graduate with job-ready operational competencies:

Rural Horticultural Work Experience (RHWE)

Students spend structured time residing in rural farming communities, studying local horticultural practices, diagnosing field pest and disease issues, and advising growers on modern techniques.

Experiential Learning Programme (ELP)

Students work in teams managing live commercial units such as polyhouses, tissue culture labs, mushroom cultivation, or fruit processing plants, managing production, packaging, and commercial sales.

Orchard & Nursery Training

Hands-on mastery of budding, grafting, layering, pruning, training, potting mixtures, and certified nursery raising on university research farms.

Eligibility criteria & subject prerequisites

To apply for admission to B.Sc. (Hons.) Horticulture, candidates must fulfill the prescribed academic qualifications and subject requirements:

  • Pass in 10+2 (Senior Secondary) examination or equivalent from a recognised board with English, Physics, Chemistry, and Biology/Mathematics or Agriculture.
  • For ICAR all-India quota seats via CUET-UG, candidates must have passed 10+2 with at least 50% aggregate marks (40% for SC, ST, PwBD, and third-gender categories).
  • Candidates must be at least 16 years of age on 31 August of the admission year for ICAR quota admissions.
  • State-specific domicile and eligibility rules apply for state quota seats in state horticultural and agricultural universities (e.g. Dr YSRHU in Andhra Pradesh, SKLTSHU in Telangana, Dr YSPUHF in Himachal Pradesh).

Admission routes & entrance examinations

Admissions are conducted through national, state-level and university-specific competitive selection channels:

CUET-UG (ICAR All-India Quota)

Online national testing conducted by NTA followed by ICAR central counselling for 20% all-India quota seats in state universities and 100% seats in central universities.

State Horticultural / Agricultural Entrance Examinations

State-level counselling based on state CETs such as AP EAPCET, TS EAMCET, KCET, MHT-CET, UPCATET, BCECE, or KEAM for 80% state quota seats.

Direct / University-Level Entrance

Private universities and deemed institutions conduct university-level entrance tests or admit on qualifying 10+2 merit scores.

Step-by-step admission journey

  1. Check Entrance Criteria: Verify whether your chosen universities admit via CUET-UG (ICAR quota) or through state CET counselling.
  2. Appear for Entrance: Take the relevant national or state entrance test with appropriate domain subjects (PCB/PCM/PCA).
  3. Register for Online Counselling: Submit choice preferences for horticulture colleges and campuses during the official counselling window.
  4. Document Verification & Fee Payment: Verify academic certificates, category proof, and pay admission fees at the allotted college.

Fee structure & scholarships guide

Fees vary significantly between government state horticultural universities and private institutions. State horticultural universities offer subsidised tuition (typically ₹15,000 to ₹45,000 per year for regular government quota seats), whereas private universities and self-financing quotas range between ₹80,000 to ₹2,50,000 per year. Hostel, mess, and examination fees are charged separately.

Career opportunities and professional pathways

India is the second-largest producer of fruits and vegetables globally, creating substantial employment across precision farming, seed production, post-harvest supply chains, government agricultural departments, and landscape development.

Horticulture Development Officer (HDO)

State government department officer implementing agricultural development schemes, orchard subsidies, and grower advisory services.

Plantation & Estate Manager

Supervises operations on commercial tea, coffee, rubber, cardamom, or oil palm estates.

Greenhouse / Polyhouse Production Specialist

Manages high-tech protected cultivation of exotic vegetables, cut flowers, and strawberry crops.

Landscape Designer & Urban Horticulturist

Plans and manages urban landscaping, golf courses, botanical gardens, and residential greenscapes.

Post-Harvest & Cold Chain Executive

Oversees grading, cold storage, packaging, and supply-chain logistics for retail chains and export firms.

Seed Production Specialist

Oversees hybrid seed production, quality testing, and field certification for seed multinationals.

Applicant questions answered

Frequently asked questions regarding admission, course syllabus, accreditation and career prospects:

What is the difference between B.Sc. Agriculture and B.Sc. Horticulture?

B.Sc. Agriculture covers the broader scope of field crops, agronomy, soil science, farm machinery, livestock, and crop protection. B.Sc. Horticulture specialises deeply in high-value crops: fruits, vegetables, flowers, spices, plantation crops, protected greenhouse cultivation, and post-harvest technology.

Are B.Sc. Horticulture graduates eligible for IBPS AFO and state agriculture exams?

Yes. The Institute of Banking Personnel Selection (IBPS) explicitly lists B.Sc. Horticulture as an eligible qualifying degree for the Agricultural Field Officer (AFO) Scale-I recruitment. Most state agricultural exams also accept horticulture graduates alongside agriculture graduates.

Can I take admission with Physics, Chemistry, and Mathematics (PCM)?

Yes. Most national and state councils, including ICAR for CUET-UG, permit candidates with PCM in Class 12 to apply for B.Sc. Horticulture, though students must complete remedial biology coursework in their first semester.

What is the role of the RHWE programme in B.Sc. Horticulture?

Rural Horticultural Work Experience (RHWE) is an intensive semester-long hands-on training module in the final year where students live in farming villages, assist growers with pest and disease management, and study regional supply chains firsthand.

Which are the prominent specialised horticultural universities in India?

Prominent specialised state universities include Dr Yashwant Singh Parmar University of Horticulture and Forestry (Solan, HP), Dr Y.S.R. Horticultural University (Andhra Pradesh), Sri Konda Laxman Telangana State Horticultural University (Telangana), and University of Horticultural Sciences (Bagalkot, Karnataka).
