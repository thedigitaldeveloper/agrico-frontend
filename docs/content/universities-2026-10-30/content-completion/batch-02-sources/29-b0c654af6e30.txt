The School of Agriculture is an
ICAR-Accredited institution
, dedicated to shaping the next generation of agtech pioneers, researchers, and agribusiness entrepreneurs. Our industry-aligned curriculum integrates core scientific principles with advanced modern technologies—including precision farming, remote sensing applications, and smart irrigation systems—to address the global challenge of sustainable food security.
ICAR-Aligned Curriculum:
Academic tracks at the Undergraduate (B.Sc. Hons.), Postgraduate (M.Sc.), and Doctoral (Ph.D.) levels are strictly designed around modern regulatory benchmarks, ensuring high global employability and research relevance.
Advanced Research Infrastructure:
Students move beyond the classroom to train in state-of-the-art facilities, including the region's pioneering Hydroponics Working Laboratory, specialized Soil Science testing setups, and advanced Plant Pathology units.
Experiential & Field-Based Learning:
Through our dedicated instructional farms and protected cultivation polyhouses, theory translates directly into practice. Students master everything from crop production management to advanced Integrated Pest Management (IPM) strategies.
Entrepreneurship & Global Networks:
We foster a robust ecosystem for student innovation, preparing graduates for high-impact roles in agribusiness management, agro-pharmaceutical sectors, government research bodies, or independent agritech startups.

Are classes taught by professors or teaching assistants?
All courses at the School of Agriculture are delivered exclusively by experienced Professors and
                        Assistant Professors using multimedia teaching tools, modern projectors, and high-definition
                        smart boards.
What is the average class size and delivery structure?
Student cohorts are maintained in Sections A, B, and C with an optimal strength of 30–35
                        students per class. Faculty members conduct dedicated Q&A discussions after lectures and
                        structured revision sessions after syllabus completion.
What academic support and mentoring systems are available?
The School operates a dedicated Proctorial System where each student is assigned a faculty
                        mentor at a 1:10 ratio. Weekly mentoring sessions support academic progress, field learning, and
                        student well-being.
What is the academic workload outside lectures?
Practical learning is intensive, with mandatory laboratory and field sessions alongside theory
                        classes. Advanced students also complete Experiential Learning Programmes (ELP) involving
                        multi-disciplinary field operations.
What are the eligibility and entrance criteria?
Undergraduate admission requires 10+2 with PCM/PCB or Agriculture through JET or ICAR channels.
                        M.Sc. admission requires B.Sc. Agriculture through SGVUEE, while Ph.D. admissions are conducted
                        through the university research entrance framework.
Does the School have farms and field laboratories?
Yes. The department manages instructional farms, climate-controlled polyhouses, and advanced
                        field laboratories for practical training in agronomy, horticulture, and soil testing.
Are internships and corporate field training available?
Yes. Students complete continuous field exposure and mandatory RAWE programmes, along with
                        industrial attachments and agribusiness internships.
Does the university provide placement and competitive exam support?
Yes. The School coordinates with the Training & Placement Office for placement assistance
                        and provides guidance for ICAR-JRF/SRF, NET, NABARD, IBPS AFO, and other competitive
                        examinations.
