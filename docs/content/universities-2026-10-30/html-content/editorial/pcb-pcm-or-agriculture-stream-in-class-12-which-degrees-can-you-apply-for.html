How subject rules differ between B.Sc. Agriculture and B.Tech. Agricultural Engineering

If you completed Class 12 with Physics, Chemistry and Biology (PCB) or Agriculture, your primary undergraduate options are B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture and B.Sc. (Hons.) Horticulture. By contrast, B.Tech. Agricultural Engineering is an engineering degree that requires Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics (PCM) across virtually all state and private universities.

For B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture itself, subject acceptance varies by institution: check the programme eligibility tables for Vellore Institute of Technology, Siksha ‘O’ Anusandhan and Kalasalingam Academy of Research and Education alongside B.Sc. Agriculture or Agricultural Engineering: compare the study paths.

Why first-year curricula include introductory biology and mathematics

Under the ICAR Deans’ Committee curriculum framework, students entering B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture from a Biology background take Introductory Mathematics in the first year, while students entering from a Mathematics background (where permitted) take Introductory Biology. Read B.Sc. Agriculture eligibility: subjects and admission routes before applying.

Questions answered

Can a PCB student without Mathematics apply for B.Tech. Agricultural Engineering?

Generally no. B.Tech. Agricultural Engineering requires Class 12 Mathematics (PCM/PCMB). Always verify the exact university prospectus.

Does every university accept PCM (without Biology) for B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture?

No. Some universities accept both PCB and PCM/PCMB, while others require Biology or Agriculture in Class 12. Check the institution’s specific eligibility table on Agrico.
