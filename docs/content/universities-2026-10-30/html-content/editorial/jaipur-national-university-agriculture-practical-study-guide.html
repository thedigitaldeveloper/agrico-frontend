Start with the qualification you would actually join

Jaipur National University is a self-financed private institution sponsored by Mahima Shiksha Samiti. Its School of Agricultural Sciences presents agricultural study within the university’s wider range of programmes. The agriculture undergraduate listing gives a four-year honours degree and a selection description of JET plus personal interview.

The qualification determines the subjects, practical work and entry route you need to compare. This guide focuses on B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture at Jaipur, Rajasthan.

B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture4 yearsThe official UG listing states 10+2 with minimum 50% in Science/Agriculture. The subject-combination text is incomplete on the page; obtain the current prospectus before assessing individual eligibility.

Connect the learning activities with your own interests

The published agriculture outline includes hands-on subject modules and describes visits to research and agricultural institutions. This is curricular information, not a guarantee of a particular destination or visit for every student.

Consider which of the documented activities you would like to practise and how they relate to the degree you are applying for. Use this list in a department discussion about the actual timetable and supervision.

  • The published eighth-semester list includes seed production, mushroom cultivation and soil/plant/water/seed testing modules.
  • The course page describes educational visits to agricultural research, extension and industry settings.

Agriculture practical facilities identified in FAQ

The school FAQ identifies soil-science, entomology and crop-physiology laboratory work alongside agronomy fields, a horticulture orchard and farm machinery. This is a source list of learning settings, not a verified capacity or access schedule.

Prepare for the published admission route

JNU Jaipur’s agriculture listing gives a 50% Science/Agriculture threshold and JET plus personal interview. The university also runs a wider scholarship-test process; do not assume that this replaces the agriculture-specific selection route.
