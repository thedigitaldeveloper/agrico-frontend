VCI clinical licensing versus ICAR agricultural education

A Bachelor of Veterinary Science and Animal Husbandry (B.V.Sc. & A.H.)—such as the programme profiled at Khalsa College of Veterinary and Animal Sciences under GADVASU, Ludhiana—spans five and a half years, including a compulsory one-year rotational clinical and livestock internship, and is governed by the Veterinary Council of India (VCI) minimum standards.

By contrast, B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture at institutions like Khalsa College, Amritsar or Guru Kashi University is a four-year (eight-semester) degree focused on agronomy, soil science, genetics, plant protection, agricultural economics and final-year RAWE/ELP field modules.

Comparing NEET-UG/university veterinary counselling with agriculture entry routes

Veterinary admissions typically require Physics, Chemistry, Biology and English in Class 12 alongside NEET-UG or state veterinary university counselling (such as GADVASU), and carry distinct clinical and management-quota fee structures. Questions answered

Can a B.Sc. (Hons.) Agriculture graduate practice as a licensed veterinarian?

No. Veterinary clinical practice and registration with a State Veterinary Council require a recognised B.V.Sc. & A.H. qualification under the Indian Veterinary Council Act.

Why is B.V.Sc. & A.H. 5.5 years long while B.Sc. Agriculture is 4 years?
